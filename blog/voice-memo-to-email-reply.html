Voice Memo to Email Reply: A Faster Workflow for Solo Consultants

For solo consultants who think out loud and reply in writing · 5 min read

As a solo consultant you're the whole company. You think out loud into a voice memo on the walk between meetings, then you sit down to turn that into a client email — and lose ten minutes reconstructing what you meant.

The two-format tax

You operate in two formats: spoken (fast, you record thoughts on the go) and written (slow, the client needs an email). The tax is the translation between them. Most consultants pay it manually every single time.

A tighter loop

  1. Record the voice memo as usual — the client update, the answer to their question, the next step.
  2. Convert it to text immediately. Don't "do it later"; later is where voice memos go to die.
  3. Let a draft reply form from the text — confirm the key points, ask only what's missing, keep your tone.
  4. Edit the two rough spots and hit send.

Steps 2 and 3 are where the time lives. If a tool does the transcription and drafts the reply, you've removed the rewrite-from-scratch tax and kept only the part that's actually you — the judgment.

Why this matters more for solo consultants

When you're solo, there's no assistant to "take it from here." Every minute you spend decoding your own voice memo is a minute not spent on the work the client pays for. Compounding that across a day of messages is real money.
